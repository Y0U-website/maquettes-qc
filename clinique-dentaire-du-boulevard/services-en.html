Dental Abscess and Infection

What Is a Dental Abscess?

A dental abscess is a pocket of pus caused by a bacterial infection inside the tooth or in the surrounding gum tissue. It is one of the most painful dental conditions a patient can experience and should always be treated as an urgent matter. Unlike many other dental issues, an abscess does not resolve on its own and will continue to worsen if left untreated.

In severe cases, a dental infection can spread beyond the mouth to the jaw, neck, or other parts of the body, making prompt treatment not just important but essential.

What Are the Symptoms?

The symptoms of a dental abscess are typically hard to ignore. They include severe, throbbing toothache that may radiate to the jaw, ear, or neck, sensitivity to hot and cold, pain when biting or chewing, swelling of the face or cheek, swollen and tender lymph nodes under the jaw, fever, and a sudden rush of foul-tasting fluid in the mouth if the abscess ruptures.

If you are experiencing any combination of these symptoms, treat it as an emergency and contact the clinic right away.

What Causes a Dental Abscess?

A dental abscess most commonly develops as a result of untreated tooth decay that has progressed deep enough to reach the pulp of the tooth, allowing bacteria to infect the inner tissue. It can also develop following trauma to a tooth, as a complication of gum disease, or around a tooth that has a failed or incomplete restoration.

The underlying cause must always be identified and treated in addition to addressing the abscess itself, otherwise the infection is likely to return.

What to Do in a Dental Emergency

If you suspect you have a dental abscess, do not wait for the pain to pass on its own. Take over-the-counter pain medication such as ibuprofen to manage discomfort in the meantime and contact the clinic immediately. Do not apply heat to the area, as this can encourage the infection to spread.

If you experience difficulty breathing or swallowing, swelling that is spreading rapidly, or a high fever alongside dental pain, go to the emergency room immediately as these may be signs that the infection has spread.

Treatment at the Clinic

Treatment depends on the severity of the infection. In many cases, a root canal is performed to remove the infected pulp and save the tooth, followed by a course of antibiotics to clear the infection. If the tooth cannot be saved, extraction may be necessary. In some cases, the abscess may need to be drained before definitive treatment can take place.

Do not wait until the pain becomes unbearable. The earlier an abscess is treated, the simpler and more predictable the outcome. Call us now at (514) 277-7737.

Jaw Pain

What Is Jaw Pain?

Jaw pain is a broad term that can refer to discomfort, stiffness, clicking, or limited movement in the jaw joint or surrounding muscles. It is more common than most people realize and can range from a mild, occasional nuisance to a debilitating condition that affects eating, speaking, and sleeping.

Jaw pain should never be dismissed as something you simply have to live with. In most cases, it has a clear underlying cause that can be identified and treated effectively.

What Causes Jaw Pain?

The most common cause of jaw pain is a condition called temporomandibular joint disorder, commonly referred to as TMJ or TMD. This occurs when the joint connecting the lower jaw to the skull becomes inflamed, misaligned, or damaged, causing pain, clicking sounds, and restricted movement.

Other common causes of jaw pain include bruxism (teeth grinding and clenching), stress-related muscle tension, dental infections, a misaligned bite, arthritis, or trauma to the jaw. In some cases, jaw pain is referred pain originating from a tooth, ear, or sinus issue.

What Are the Symptoms?

Symptoms associated with jaw pain and TMD include pain or tenderness in the jaw joint, aching pain in and around the ear, difficulty or discomfort while chewing, a clicking, popping, or grating sound when opening or closing the mouth, a feeling that the jaw is locked or difficult to open fully, and headaches or neck pain that accompany jaw discomfort.

If you are experiencing any of these symptoms regularly, it is important to have your jaw evaluated by a dentist.

What to Do in a Dental Emergency

If you experience sudden, severe jaw pain, difficulty opening or closing your mouth, or your jaw feels locked in place, contact the clinic immediately. In the meantime, apply a warm compress to the affected area to help relax the muscles, eat soft foods only, and avoid wide mouth movements such as yawning or chewing hard foods.

Do not attempt to force your jaw open or closed if it feels locked, as this can cause further injury to the joint.

Treatment at the Clinic

Treatment for jaw pain depends on the underlying cause. For bruxism-related jaw pain, a custom night guard or therapeutic Botox injections may be recommended. For TMD, treatment may include occlusal adjustments, physiotherapy referral, anti-inflammatory medication, or a combination of approaches.

If jaw pain is affecting your daily life, do not put off seeking care. Contact us at (514) 277-7737 to book an evaluation and we will get to the bottom of it.

Tooth Sensitivity

What Is Tooth Sensitivity?

Tooth sensitivity, also known as dentin hypersensitivity, is a sharp, sudden pain or discomfort triggered by certain stimuli such as hot or cold foods and drinks, sweet or acidic foods, cold air, or even brushing. It is one of the most common complaints in dentistry and affects patients of all ages.

While sensitivity is often dismissed as a minor inconvenience, it can be a sign of an underlying dental issue that deserves attention. Identifying and addressing the cause is always more effective than simply masking the pain.

What Causes Tooth Sensitivity?

Sensitivity occurs when the dentin, the layer of tooth structure beneath the enamel, becomes exposed. Dentin contains microscopic tubules that lead directly to the nerve of the tooth, and when these tubules are exposed to external stimuli, the nerve responds with a sharp sensation.

Common causes of dentin exposure include enamel erosion from acidic foods and drinks, gum recession that exposes the root surface, aggressive brushing, teeth grinding, cracked teeth, cavities, and worn or leaking fillings. Sometimes sensitivity develops temporarily following a dental procedure and resolves on its own within a few days.

When Should You Be Concerned?

Occasional mild sensitivity to very cold or very hot foods is common and not necessarily a cause for alarm. However, sensitivity that is severe, persistent, affects a specific tooth, or is getting worse over time should always be evaluated by a dentist.

These patterns can indicate a cavity, a cracked tooth, gum recession, or an issue with an existing restoration that needs to be addressed before it progresses further.

What to Do in a Dental Emergency

If you experience sudden, severe sensitivity or sharp pain in a specific tooth that does not subside within a few seconds of removing the stimulus, contact the clinic as soon as possible. This type of pain, especially when accompanied by swelling, spontaneous throbbing, or pain that wakes you at night, may indicate an infection or abscess that requires prompt treatment.

In the meantime, avoid the foods and temperatures that trigger the pain and take over-the-counter pain medication if needed for comfort.

Treatment at the Clinic

Treatment for tooth sensitivity depends entirely on the cause. Options include desensitizing treatments applied directly to the tooth surface, fluoride applications to strengthen enamel, bonding to cover exposed root surfaces, a custom night guard if grinding is the culprit, or restorative treatment if a cavity or damaged filling is involved.

If sensitivity is affecting your quality of life, do not ignore it. Contact us at (514) 277-7737 to book an appointment and we will identify the cause and recommend the most effective solution for your situation.

Dental Trauma

What Is Dental Trauma?

Dental trauma refers to any injury affecting the teeth, gums, lips, jaw, or surrounding structures as a result of an impact or accident. It can happen to anyone at any age, whether from a sports injury, a fall, a car accident, or any other sudden physical impact. Dental trauma ranges from a chipped or cracked tooth to a tooth that has been completely knocked out.

Regardless of the severity, any dental trauma should be evaluated by a dentist as soon as possible. What appears to be a minor injury on the surface can sometimes involve damage that is not immediately visible, including injury to the root or surrounding bone.

Common Types of Dental Trauma

The most common forms of dental trauma include chipped or fractured teeth, teeth that have been pushed deeper into the gum or displaced out of their normal position, and teeth that have been completely knocked out, a situation called avulsion. Soft tissue injuries to the lips, tongue, cheeks, and gums are also common and may accompany tooth injuries.

In children, dental trauma can also affect the development of permanent teeth if the injury involves a baby tooth and the underlying tissue is damaged.

What to Do in a Dental Emergency

If a tooth has been knocked out completely, handle it by the crown only and never touch the root. If possible, gently rinse it with milk or saline solution and try to reinsert it into the socket. If reinsertion is not possible, keep the tooth in a glass of milk or between your cheek and gum to preserve it while you make your way to the clinic.

For chipped, cracked, or displaced teeth, rinse your mouth gently with warm water, apply a cold compress to the outside of the face to reduce swelling, and contact the clinic immediately. Time is critical in dental trauma situations, and the sooner you are seen, the better the chances of saving the tooth.

Treatment at the Clinic

Treatment for dental trauma depends entirely on the nature and severity of the injury. A chipped tooth may only require bonding or a simple restoration. A displaced tooth may need to be repositioned and stabilized with a temporary splint. A knocked-out tooth that is brought in quickly enough may be successfully reimplanted.

Your dentist will take X-rays to assess the full extent of the injury, including any damage below the gum line, and establish the most appropriate treatment plan to restore your tooth and protect your long-term oral health.

Dental trauma is a true emergency. The sooner you contact the clinic, the greater the chances of a successful outcome. If you or someone in your care has experienced a dental injury, call us immediately at (514) 277-7737. We will do everything we can to see you as quickly as possible.
