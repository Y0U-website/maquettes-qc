An inlay is a restoration made in the laboratory to repair a tooth. There are different materials for making an inlay: gold, porcelain and resin. Since gold is more durable, it is less aesthetic than porcelain. The choice of material is made according to the wishes of the patient and the recommendations of the dentist. If the extent of the repair is very large, the dentist might suggest using a crown to care for the tooth instead.

An inlay can last between 5 and 20 years, depending mainly on the maintenance, the material with which it is made and oral habits (grinding your teeth or biting your nails shorten the life of an inlay).

In most cases, the natural color of the teeth varies from grayish to yellow. Teeth naturally darken with age, and the use of tobacco products and the consumption of certain foods or drinks can affect their appearance by causing stains on their surface.

In addition, the perception of tooth color is strongly affected by skin color and makeup. Regardless of the actual color of their teeth, people with darker skin or who use dark makeup will appear to have whiter teeth. Although teeth are not made naturally to be completely white, many Canadians want a brighter smile. To meet this desire, a wide range of bleaching products has been made available to consumers. These products fall into two broad categories: teeth whitening products and whitening products.

It is important to note that Health Canada considers the statements of manufacturers of teeth whitening products to be cosmetic in nature. This means that, although the statement must be accurate in order not to mislead the public, the regulatory body tolerates a certain exaggeration (exaggeration which must not mislead the public). As a result, the results may not be as convincing as the consumer initially hoped.
