Dental Implants in Waterloo

  • 2 Placement The implant — a small titanium post — is placed in the jaw in one appointment, usually under local freezing. Most people are back at work the next day; expect a few days of tenderness managed with over-the-counter pain relief.
  • 3 Healing Bone grows onto the implant over roughly three to six months. This waiting period is what makes the result stable — it can’t be rushed, and we’ll tell you honestly if your case needs longer.
  • 4 The final tooth Once healed, we take a digital scan and fit a custom crown, bridge, or denture onto the implant. It’s checked against your bite and adjusted until it feels like yours.

Questions people actually ask

The placement itself is done under local anaesthetic, so you shouldn’t feel sharp pain during the appointment. Afterwards, most people describe a few days of soreness — like a tooth extraction — managed with over-the-counter pain relief. We’ll tell you what to expect for your specific case.

Typically three to six months from placement to final tooth, because the implant needs time to fuse with bone. Some cases take longer; if yours will, we’ll say so at the planning stage, not midway through.

With good home care and regular checkups, implants commonly last decades. They can’t get cavities, but the gums around them still need cleaning — we’ll show you how.

Age alone rarely rules anyone out. What matters is bone volume, gum health, and general health — all things we assess at the planning visit.

Coverage varies widely by plan. Bring your plan details and we’ll check before you commit — and every estimate we give is in writing.

Bone grafting can rebuild the site in many cases. It adds time to the process, and we’ll walk you through what it involves before you decide.
