Questions people actually ask

Every six months is a common rhythm, but the right interval depends on your gums, not a fixed rule. Some people do well on longer; some need three or four months. We’ll suggest an interval based on what we actually see, and revisit it as things change.

Most people describe scraping and vibration rather than pain. If your gums are inflamed, or there’s a lot of tartar to remove, some spots will be tender — say so and we can slow down, take breaks, or numb the area. Tenderness afterwards usually settles within a day or two.

Bleeding is usually a sign of early gum inflammation — gingivitis — caused by plaque sitting at the gumline. It’s common, and it’s usually reversible with a professional cleaning and steady home care. It’s worth having looked at, because the later stages of gum disease are harder to turn around.
